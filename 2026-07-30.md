Later that afternoon, a question from Nate about where a citation link would actually
point once the shop's repos went live turned up a second hole — this one in how the
composer's own citations got verified.

**3:17 PM — MACHINE:**

> Direct answer to your question: as phrased there — *"those are published too, day by day, at [repo root]"* — the repo root is actually the *correct* link. That sentence is a "go browse the whole collection" pointer, not a claim about one specific exchange, and the repo root is exactly where you browse all the days.
> [...]
> So: **once stood up properly, a reference to a specific exchange should be the day-file link; the repo root stays valid only for "here's the whole archive."** Two things stand between here and that being reliable:
>
> 1. **The contract doesn't yet tell it to prefer the most specific file.** Easy fix — one line in `WRITER-CONTRACT.md`: *cite the exact file you actually opened, by its real path; use the bare repo root only when you explicitly mean "browse the whole collection."*
> 2. **The real risk isn't root-vs-file — it's that the composer builds these URLs itself from the room, and nothing verifies they resolve.** The gate (G6) only checks the *domain* is on the allowlist, not that the link 200s. A composer could construct a plausible-but-dead `blob/main/...` permalink and the gate would pass it. That's the citation-accuracy gap I flagged for the ceremony, and it's the one worth closing mechanically.

**3:20 PM — NATE:**

> do the writer contract now, and yep, let's get number two fixed as well. Today is the day I'm getting this ready for primetime.

**3:24 PM — MACHINE:**

> That's a more important discovery than the gate itself: **natestpierre.me returns 200 for missing pages** (Cloudflare Pages serves the home page as a soft-404), so live HTTP status alone can't verify an archive link — a guessed archive URL would pass.
