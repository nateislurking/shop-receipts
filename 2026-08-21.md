## The Gmail connector's dead handshake, finally explained

Nate had noticed the connector going dark every morning for a while. He finally asked why, mid-conversation.

**7:34 AM — NATE:**

> why does Gmail connector drop literally every night?

**7:35 AM — MACHINE:**

> Honest answer with confidence labels, because I can't see the connector's server logs — but the pattern is well-documented in this shop:
>
> **The mechanism (verified in our own records for the same auth class):** the Gmail connector is a claude.ai-hosted MCP server that this session reaches with OAuth credentials, and that class of token **expires after ~8 hours and only refreshes on interactive use**. [...] Your desk sessions run long and then sit idle overnight; nothing interactive touches the connector during those hours; the token ages out; and when the session wakes the next morning, the handshake fails and the harness marks all 36 Gmail/Calendar tools gone. So it's not that something *breaks* every night — it's that the credential is designed to die without a human touching it, and overnight is precisely when no human touches it.
>
> **Why the rest of the shop doesn't have this problem:** every standing lane already learned this lesson and got its own durable credential — the reply lane reads mail with its own Fastmail JMAP token, sends through Resend, the digest sends through MailerLite. None of them depend on the connector, which is why they run at 4am without me.

A second, unrelated symptom came up right after — the connect button itself failing before it works.

**7:35 AM — NATE:**

> And then when I try to use the connector button on Claude's settings, it says "fail to connect, fail to connect", and then eventually it connects.

**7:35 AM — MACHINE:**

> That part's entirely on Anthropic's side of the fence — the button in settings kicks off an OAuth re-flow plus a handshake to their hosted connector service, and "fail, fail, then works" is the signature of a cold-start or a race in that handshake: the first click wakes something up, the retries land once it's warm. [...] the fact that it *does* eventually connect means your Google authorization itself is fine; it's their plumbing being sluggish, not your credential being broken.
