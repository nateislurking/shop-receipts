## A storage alert that wasn't

A 100%-storage warning from GitHub turned into a question about which meter was actually full.

**3:39 PM — MACHINE:**

> ## Your two questions
>
> **Do simple pushes pull from the Actions allowance?** No. Not at all. Git pushes count as *repository* storage, which is a completely separate meter. "Actions storage" only measures **workflow artifacts** (and Packages). Nothing you do with `git push` — this session's eight pushes included — touches that number.
>
> **Do you need to enable payment to keep pushing?** No. Pushing is unaffected either way. The only thing at risk is **the Mac release workflow's artifact upload step**. If a $0 budget is set, that step fails on the next Mac cut; if no budget is set, you get billed a small overage. Your repos, your pushes, your backups at `/wrap` — all fine, no payment needed.

**3:41 PM — NATE:**

> ## Why "0.5 GB available" but 1.86 GB sitting there
>
> **0.5 GB isn't a cap. It's the amount included free each cycle.** GitHub happily lets you go past it and bills the overage — the alert says exactly that: *"You'll be billed for usage beyond the included amount — unless a $0 budget is set for Actions, which blocks further usage."*

> This is the part worth nailing down: **there is no "repo storage" bill on your plan.** The only metered storage lines GitHub charges for are **Actions/Packages storage** and **Git LFS**. Ordinary git content — every push you've ever made — isn't metered at all.
